UNDERSTANDING BROWSER FINGERPRINTING

What is it?

Browser fingerprinting identifies a device by collecting many technical attributes instead of storing a cookie. Each attribute is common on its own, but the combination is often unique.

Because nothing is saved on your device, clearing cookies or using private mode does not remove it.

What this test measures

  • Canvas: pixels produced by your GPU, drivers and fonts
  • WebGL: graphics card vendor, renderer and limits
  • Audio: how your audio stack processes a signal
  • Fonts: which common fonts are installed
  • Hardware & display: screen, CPU cores, memory, touch
  • Locale & privacy signals: language, time zone, DNT, GPC

How the score works

Each attribute is assigned an approximate number of bits of identifying information, based on typical values from public fingerprinting research. The bits are added together to estimate how identifiable the combination is.

Treat it as an educational estimate: many attributes are correlated, so the real number is usually lower, and a true population measurement would need a large dataset of visitors.

Randomization detection

The test draws the same hidden canvas and audio signal twice. If the results differ, your browser adds random noise, which is how Brave and Firefox's strict protection modes defeat fingerprinting.

Tor Browser takes the opposite approach and makes every user look identical.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Does this test store or send my data?

No. The analysis runs locally in your browser. Only the optional IP and WebRTC tests make external requests, and only after you click them.

Is browser fingerprinting legal?

Rules vary by country. In the EU, accessing information on your device for tracking generally requires consent under ePrivacy and GDPR, but enforcement against fingerprinting is uneven.

Will a VPN hide my fingerprint?

No. A VPN changes your IP address, but the rest of the fingerprint (screen, fonts, GPU, canvas) stays the same.

Why does my fingerprint ID change?

Updating your browser, changing screen, installing fonts, or enabling a privacy extension changes the attributes and therefore the ID.

How can I become harder to track?

Use a browser with built-in fingerprint protection (Brave, Firefox with resistFingerprinting, or Tor Browser), keep extensions to a minimum, avoid unusual fonts, and keep the browser updated.
